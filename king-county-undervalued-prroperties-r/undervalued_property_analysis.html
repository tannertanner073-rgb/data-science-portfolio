King County Undervalued Property Analysis

Problem Statement

This project explores whether a simple pricing model can help identify homes that appear underpriced relative to their estimated market value.

Instead of only asking, “What drives housing prices?”, I wanted to ask a more strategy-focused question:

Which properties appear underpriced compared to what their features suggest they should be worth?

The goal is to:

  • Build a pricing benchmark model
  • Compare predicted prices against actual sale prices
  • Identify properties with large positive pricing gaps
  • Treat the results as screening signals, not guaranteed investment opportunities

Pricing Model

Purpose: Build a simple baseline model to estimate a home’s expected price based on key observable features.

model <- lm(price ~ sqft_living + grade + bathrooms + view + waterfront, data = housing)

Code concept:
lm() builds a linear regression model that estimates price as a combination of selected features.
Each variable contributes independently to the predicted value.

Why these features?
I focused on variables that are: - Intuitively important to pricing (size, quality, amenities) - Easy to interpret - Likely to capture a large portion of price variation

Insight:
This model is not meant to be perfect — it acts as a pricing benchmark.
The real value comes from comparing its predictions to actual sale prices.

Identify Undervalued Properties

Purpose: Compare predicted prices to actual prices to identify homes that may be underpriced.

# Predict prices
housing$predicted_price <- predict(model, newdata = housing)

# Calculate pricing gap
housing$price_difference <- housing$predicted_price - housing$price

# Sort by most undervalued
underpriced <- housing[order(-housing$price_difference), ]

Code concept:
- predict() generates model-estimated prices
- price_difference measures mispricing
- Sorting highlights the largest positive gaps (predicted > actual)

Strategic Interpretation

What the Model Reveals:

The regression model captures key drivers of housing prices such as square footage, grade, and location-related features. With an R² of approximately 0.59, the model explains a meaningful portion of price variation, though not all.

Undervalued Properties:

Properties identified with large positive price differences (predicted > actual) may represent potential undervaluation. These properties appear to have characteristics that the market may not have fully priced in.

Key Insight:

Undervaluation is not purely random — it often appears in properties where: - Strong physical attributes (size, grade) exist - But other factors (timing, seller urgency, condition, or missing variables) may have suppressed the sale price

Important Limitation:

This model does not capture all real-world factors such as: - Interior condition - Renovation quality - Market timing - Buyer/seller negotiation dynamics

Therefore, results should be interpreted as signals, not definitive investment opportunities.

Strategic Use Case:

This type of model could be used by: - Real estate investors to screen opportunities
- Agents to identify pricing inefficiencies
- Analysts to benchmark listing prices

Decision Framing

This model is not designed to identify guaranteed investment opportunities, but rather to prioritize where deeper investigation should be focused. The strongest value comes from narrowing a large market into a smaller set of high-potential candidates. The key value is not predicting price perfectly, but identifying relative mispricing patterns.
